    How the duel works
    • Every blot is a die. Its six sides come from the blot itself: stars (★ with its level) and symbols from its stats. Flow gives Step, Opacity gives Brush, Saturation gives Splash, Grain gives Guard, Glow gives Glow. A very high stat gives one doubled side. The net on the card is how the die unfolds.
    • Your bag holds six dice: the two weakest are level 1, the two strongest level 3.
    • Each turn you pick three dice and roll them. Symbols go to your pool and stay there.
    • Stars summon: a blot can come out if the other dice show stars worth at least its level. Its die unfolds onto the sheet as six cells of wet paint, touching your own paint, and the blot stands on it. At most three blots stand on the sheet.
    • Then each blot on the sheet may move once (1 Step) and act once: Dry stamp (1 Brush), Wet stamp (1 Brush and 1 water), Splash (1 Splash), its ability (2 Glow) or Soak (free: heals, +1 water).
    • Blots only travel through paint. Bare paper is a wall, except for flyers and hoppers. Own paint costs 1, rival paint 2, mud stops you.
    • Splash: Saturation plus a d20 against Grain plus a d20. Complementary colours hit for 1.5 times and stain, harmonious for 0.75. A Guard in the pool halves a hit by itself.
    • Each side has a palette with three hearts. A splash on the rival palette always takes a heart. Break all three and you win.
    • A washed-out blot loses its die for good, so your rolls get fewer.
    • After rounds 3 and 6 the Eraser wipes a row. After 8 rounds the paper dries: cells plus 3 per heart left.
    • Rival strength: the Apprentice weighs only a few moves and has weaker blots, the Master weighs every move.
    Every blot is unique, so is every die.
