How to play

  • Every blot is a die. Its sides come from the blot itself: stars (★ with its level) and symbols from its stats. Flow gives Step, Saturation gives Splash, Grain or Opacity gives Guard, Glow gives Glow. Tap ⓘ on a die to see its net.
  • Opening: both sides unfold one blot for free next to their palette. The net is already placed, tap it to unfold.
  • Your deck holds nine dice. Each turn pick three ready dice and roll them (I suggest three, tap to swap). Rolled dice are used up, like cards. When fewer than three are ready, the used ones are shuffled back in. A die whose blot stands on the sheet stays out of the deck. Symbols go to your pool and keep.
  • Hold and reroll: the roll lies open first. Tap dice to hold them and reroll the rest, once per turn, or keep the roll. Then it counts.
  • Combos: a pair of the same symbol gives one more of it, a triple two more and a Joker, three different symbols (a rainbow) a Joker, three stars of the same level a Joker. A Joker counts as any symbol for a combo and stays a Joker.
  • Stars summon: a blot can come out if the other dice show stars worth at least its level. Its die unfolds onto the sheet as six cells of wet paint, touching your paint. Where rival paint, a blot or a palette is in the way, the net may hang over: those sides drip off and paint nothing, as long as at least three cells land. A star that cannot summon says why under the die. At most three blots stand on the sheet.
  • One drag per blot: drag it onto paper to walk there and stamp its shape, onto a rival to splash it, onto their palette to hit it. Or tap a blot, then tap the target. Tap it twice to soak (heal, rinse stains). When every blot has acted, the turn ends by itself.
  • Moving: your own paint costs 1, bare paper, rival paint and mud cost 2, mud stops you. A Step in your pool lets one blot go 2 further.
  • Mediums: pick one of three before the duel, and again after rounds two and four. Each bends one rule for you: Gum Arabic, Ox Gall, Salt, Retarder, Sponge, Iridescent Medium, and four more that unlock as you finish duels. Tap a bottle above the sheet to read it.
  • Colour families: two blots of one family on the sheet work together. Warm (Carmine, Opera Rose, Cadmium) splash for +1, cool (Ultramarine, Indigo, Phthalo) walk one further, earth (Sienna, Sepia) heal one each round. Three of a family do more.
  • Colour reactions: the same pigment over your own dried paint glazes it, a second layer that counts double and holds better. A neighbouring colour on the colour wheel bleeds into a free cell next to it. A complementary colour turns wet rival paint into mud.
  • Stamps are wet paint and free. Over dried rival paint they need a roll: Opacity against Grain. Wet paint bleeds into free paper when the round ends, then dries.
  • Splash is free and always lands: Saturation plus a d20 against Grain plus a d20, the roll shows at the target. Win the roll for a full hit, lose it and the splash only grazes for half. A natural 20 can be a crit. Light blots dodge now and then. A hit paints the cell under the target. Complementary colours hit for 1.5 times and stain. A Guard in your pool halves a hit by itself. Two or three hits and a blot bursts, its die is lost for good.
  • Go again: a blot that washes out a rival blot acts once more right away, once per turn. Dragging onto a rival shows WASH OUT when a hit is enough.
  • Master strokes: stamping, splashing, soaking and taking hits fill a blot's brim, Glow pours in. When it is full, its next fitting move adds its ability on top. A blot that bursts at least half full spills once more.
  • Palettes: three hearts each. A blot next to the rival palette hits it with a Splash symbol: one heart, at most one per turn. A palette is safe while one of its own blots stands within two cells. Break all three and you win.
  • Gallery: after every finished duel your deck hangs in the gallery. A gallery duel finds the deck of another painter of about your strength, the rival plays it with its real dice. When their deck wins, its painter earns a small fee, and so do you when yours wins. Take yours down in the menu. Drawings of others only show once a moderator has seen them, report anything that does not belong.
  • The Eraser wipes a row after rounds 2 and 4. After 6 rounds the paper dries: cells plus 3 per heart left.
  • The gold ring and arrow show what Master Sepia would do. Sepia paints plays the rest of your turn for you. Turn hints off in the menu.
